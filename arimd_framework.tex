\section{Setting}
\label{sec:setting}

We consider a multi-agent sequential social dilemma (SSD)
$G$ with agent set $[N] = \{1, \dots, N\}$, episode horizon $T$,
and per-agent stage rewards $r_t^{(i)} \in \R$.  The cumulative
return of agent $i$ is $R_i = \sum_{t=1}^T r_t^{(i)}$.

We fix:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*), itemsep=2pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item A bounded \emph{edit grammar} $\Egram$ over the
        environment's parameters and rule toggles.  An element
        $e \in \Egram$ instantiates a concrete env $G(e)$ that
        differs from the default $G$ only along the grammar's
        listed axes.
  \item A frozen \emph{cooperator policy} $\bluepol$ — a
        hand-crafted reference behaviour, the same across runs.
  \item An adversary fraction $\lambda \in [0,1]$.  In every
        episode the agents are partitioned into a Red subset
        $\mathcal{R} \subseteq [N]$ with
        $|\mathcal{R}| = \lfloor \lambda N \rfloor$ (re-sampled
        per seed) and a Blue complement
        $\mathcal{B} = [N] \setminus \mathcal{R}$.  Red agents
        play a policy $\pi_R$ chosen by the adversary; Blue
        agents play $\bluepol$.
  \item A \emph{welfare functional} $\Welfare$ that maps the
        per-agent return vector $\mathbf{R} = (R_1, \dots, R_N)$
        to a scalar.  Default: Blue-welfare,
        $\Welfare(\mathbf{R}; \mathcal{B}) =
         \tfrac{1}{|\mathcal{B}|} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{B}} R_i$.
        Population-welfare,
        $\tfrac{1}{N}\sum_i R_i$, is reported alongside.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent The mixed-population value of an env--policy pair is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Vlambda}
  V_\lambda(e, \pi_R)
  \;=\;
  \E_{S, \mathcal{R}}
    \big[\,
      \Welfare\!\big(\,
        \mathbf{R};\; \mathcal{B}
      \,\big)
      \;\big|\;
      G(e), S, \pi_R, \bluepol
    \,\big],
\end{equation}
where the expectation is over episode seeds $S$ and the random
Red-index assignment $\mathcal{R}$.  In practice we
Monte-Carlo this with a held-out seed grid.

Red is rational and \emph{selfish}: it optimizes its own
per-capita return,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:redobj}
  \Welfare_R(\mathbf{R}; \mathcal{R})
  \;=\;
  \tfrac{1}{|\mathcal{R}|}
    \sum_{i \in \mathcal{R}} R_i.
\end{equation}

\section{The Stackelberg meta-game}
\label{sec:stackelberg}

Blue commits first to an env design; Red best-responds with the
most exploitative homogeneous-Red policy it can find.  The
\emph{adversarially-robust mechanism} is the solution of
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stackelberg}
  e^\star
  \;=\;
  \operatorname*{arg\,max}_{e \in \Egram}\;
  \min_{\pi_R \in \policyset}\;
  V_\lambda(e, \pi_R),
\end{equation}
where $\policyset$ is the space of well-formed exploit policies
(deterministic functions of the env state returning legal
actions; in our implementation, Python policies sampled from a
language model and validated by an AST-level safety check).

Compared to the symmetric pipeline-minimax
$\max_{c_B} \min_{c_R} V_\lambda(c_B, c_R)$
in which both sides choose pipeline configurations,
formulation \eqref{eq:stackelberg} gives Blue and Red
\emph{different action spaces}: Blue's lives in a small
combinatorial--continuous grammar, Red's lives in an open
program space.  This (a) eliminates inner-loop policy synthesis
on Blue's side, (b) reduces LLM-collusion risk between the two
proposers, and (c) yields an interpretable headline artifact
(the $\Egram$-diff at $e^\star$).

\paragraph{Diagnostics.}
For an accepted env $e^\star$ and the Red best-response
$\pi_R^\star$ we report three quantities:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Invasion barrier}
        $\lambda^\star =
         \sup\{\lambda : V_\lambda(e^\star, \pi_R^\star)
                       \ge V_0(e^\star, \bluepol)\}$
        — the largest adversary fraction the mechanism tolerates
        without falling below the no-attack baseline.
  \item \textbf{Exploitability}
        $\Delta_{\text{ex}}(J')
         = V_\lambda(e^\star, \pi_R^\star)
         - V_\lambda(e^\star, \tilde{\pi}_R)$,
        where $\tilde{\pi}_R$ is obtained from a fresh Red search
        of $J'$ extra iterations against frozen $e^\star$.
        $\Delta_{\text{ex}} \approx 0$ certifies near-equilibrium.
  \item \textbf{Stability gap}
        $\bigl| V_\lambda(e_t, \pi_{R,t}^\star)
              - V_\lambda(e_{t-1}, \pi_{R,t-1}^\star) \bigr|$
        across accepted Stackelberg rounds; monotone decrease
        evidences convergence.
\end{itemize}

\section{Algorithm}
\label{sec:algorithm}

ARIMD instantiates \eqref{eq:stackelberg} as a finite outer loop
with two LLM oracles: a \emph{designer} $\mathcal{D}$
(Blue) and an \emph{exploiter} $\mathcal{X}$ (Red).
$\mathcal{D}$ emits a JSON patch $\delta$ over $\Egram$;
$\mathcal{X}$ emits a Python policy.  Both are validated and
clamped before evaluation.  Acceptance is greedy: a Blue
proposal is kept only if it improves the worst-case Red value
over \emph{all} Red candidates produced in the round.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{ARIMD-Stackelberg outer loop}
\label{alg:arimd}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require env grammar $\Egram$, cooperator $\bluepol$,
         adversary fraction $\lambda$, eval seeds $S$, rounds $T$,
         Red candidates per round $M$
\Require LLM oracles $\mathcal{D}$ (Blue) and $\mathcal{X}$ (Red)
\State $e \gets e_0$ \Comment{identity patch (default env)}
\State $v^\star \gets V_\lambda(e, \bluepol)$
       \Comment{$V_0$ self-play baseline}
\State $\mathcal{H} \gets \emptyset$
       \Comment{round history shown to $\mathcal{D}$, $\mathcal{X}$}
\For{$t = 1, \dots, T$}
  \Statex \hspace*{1.5em}\textit{// Red search at frozen Blue env $e$}
  \For{$k = 1, \dots, M$}
    \State $\pi_R^{(k)} \gets \mathcal{X}(G(e), \bluepol, \mathcal{H})$
    \State $v_R^{(k)},\, v_B^{(k)}
            \gets \textsc{EvalMixed}(e, \pi_R^{(k)}, \lambda, S)$
  \EndFor
  \State $k^\star \gets \arg\max_k v_R^{(k)}$
         \Comment{selfish-defector best-response, eq.~\eqref{eq:redobj}}
  \State $\pi_R^\star \gets \pi_R^{(k^\star)}$
  \Statex \hspace*{1.5em}\textit{// Blue patch proposal}
  \State $\delta \gets \mathcal{D}\!\big(G(e), \bluepol,
              \pi_R^\star, \mathcal{H}, \Egram\big)$
  \State $e' \gets \textsc{ApplyPatch}(\Egram, e, \delta)$
  \Statex \hspace*{1.5em}\textit{// Re-evaluate against the same Red set}
  \State $v_B'
          \gets \min_{k} V_\lambda\!\big(e', \pi_R^{(k)}\big)$,
          estimated over $S$
  \If{$v_B' > v^\star$}
     \State $e \gets e'$;\;\, $v^\star \gets v_B'$
            \Comment{accept}
  \EndIf
  \State $\mathcal{H} \gets \mathcal{H} \cup
            \{(\delta,\, \pi_R^\star,\, v_B',\, v^\star)\}$
\EndFor
\State \Return $e^\star \gets e$, history $\mathcal{H}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{The mixed-population evaluator.}
\textsc{EvalMixed}$(e, \pi_R, \lambda, S)$ runs $|S|$ episodes
on $G(e)$.  Each episode draws a fresh Red index set
$\mathcal{R}$ with $|\mathcal{R}| = \lfloor \lambda N \rfloor$
uniformly at random; agents in $\mathcal{R}$ act under $\pi_R$
and the rest under $\bluepol$.  We report
$v_R = \tfrac{1}{|S|} \sum_{S} \tfrac{1}{|\mathcal{R}|}
        \sum_{i \in \mathcal{R}} R_i$
(Red's objective) and
$v_B = \tfrac{1}{|S|} \sum_{S} \tfrac{1}{|\mathcal{B}|}
        \sum_{i \in \mathcal{B}} R_i$
(Blue's welfare).  $\lambda = 0$ is a special case: $\pi_R$ is
ignored and the run measures pure Blue self-play.

\paragraph{Cost model.}
For $T$ outer rounds, $M$ Red candidates per round, $|S|$ seeds,
and one episode per seed, Algorithm~\ref{alg:arimd} runs
$T(M + M)|S|$ episodes plus $T$ Blue LLM calls and $TM$ Red LLM
calls — $\mathcal{O}(TM|S|)$ in episodes, linear in
LLM calls.  No inner policy-synthesis loop appears on Blue's side,
which is the principal source of the cost reduction over
symmetric pipeline-minimax variants.

\paragraph{Why a bounded grammar.}
$\Egram$ exposes a small, semantically meaningful action space
to $\mathcal{D}$: numeric knobs (with absolute bounds and
type-preserving clamping) and structural toggles (booleans /
small categoricals).  This is what makes the headline artifact
interpretable — $e^\star$ is a short JSON patch — and what
distinguishes ARIMD's contribution from black-box parameter
search.  If, in any given run, $\mathcal{D}$'s edits cluster on
saturated numeric bounds and ignore the structural toggles, that
is itself reportable evidence that the grammar (or the
mechanism-design problem on this env) does not require
qualitatively novel rules to defend against the chosen
$\policyset$.

\section{Implementation}
\label{sec:implementation}

We instantiate ARIMD on three sequential social dilemmas:
\texttt{nested\_commons}, a 16-agent compositional dilemma on a
$20{\times}20$ grid with intra-clan river cleaning, an inter-clan
plaza public good, and a held-inventory raiding sub-game;
\texttt{cleanup}, the standard 10-agent cleaning-vs-harvesting
public-goods env; and \texttt{production\_economy}, an 8-agent
multi-step economy used for grammar plumbing tests but not for the
trajectories below.  Edit grammars are sized for interpretability:
17 numeric knobs and 3 structural toggles for
\texttt{nested\_commons} (\texttt{plaza\_occupant\_only\_bonus},
\texttt{plaza\_local\_clean\_gate}, \texttt{same\_clan\_retaliation});
11 numerics and 1 toggle (\texttt{beam\_enabled}) for
\texttt{cleanup}.  Numeric ranges are absolute clamps centered on
each env's published defaults; toggles are booleans wired into the
env's \texttt{step()} dispatcher and gated on the relevant
config flag.

Cooperators are hand-crafted, frozen Python policies, the same
across all rounds within a run.  \texttt{nested\_commons} ships
two: an \emph{efficiency} cooperator with a static role split
(cleaners, harvesters, plaza specialists, one tile per clan), and
a \emph{maximin} cooperator with the same instantaneous role mix
but with every agent rotating through every role on a Latin-square
schedule.  \texttt{cleanup} ships one default cooperator (an
adaptive cleaner-vs-harvester role split parameterized by river
waste density).

Both LLM oracles use the framework's existing inference path.
$\mathcal{D}$ (Blue) is \texttt{claude-opus-4-7};
$\mathcal{X}$ (Red) is \texttt{gemini-3.1-pro-preview}.  Red
proposals pass an AST-level safety check and a single-episode
smoke test before evaluation; failed proposals retry up to three
times.  All trajectories below use $T = 4$ outer rounds, $M = 4$
Red candidates per round, $|S| = 5$ evaluation seeds, $\lambda =
0.25$, and welfare $\Welfare = $ Blue-mean.

\paragraph{Trajectory 1 — \texttt{nc} $\times$ maximin (no improvement).}
The maximin cooperator runs a Latin-square role rotation, so each
agent passes through every role each cycle.  Blue \emph{tried}
structural toggles in every round (each of the three
\texttt{nested\_commons} toggles appeared in at least one proposal
across the four rounds), but every patch was rejected.
Mechanism: the toggles' preconditions interact poorly with role
rotation.  The local-clean gate requires the collector's home
river to be clean at pickup time, but rotation thins the per-clan
cleaner population at any given step, so Blue forfeits its
\emph{own} plaza picks.  The occupant-only bonus requires
multiple Blue agents to be co-located in the plaza, which the
rotation does not guarantee.  The toggles bite Blue at least as
hard as they punish Red.  $V_\lambda^\star = 639.33$ in
Table~\ref{tab:headline} is the round-0 worst-Red baseline against
the unmodified env: Blue made no progress beyond seeing the first
attack.

\paragraph{Trajectory 2 — \texttt{nc} $\times$ efficiency (multi-round structural search).}
The efficiency cooperator's static specialists keep cleaners
co-located with their home quadrants and maintain $\geq 3$ Blue
agents in the plaza.  Round 0 ($V_\lambda$ after patch $= 292$;
rejected) flipped all three toggles aggressively.  Round 1 ($\to
946$; accepted) kept \texttt{same\_clan\_retaliation} and
\texttt{plaza\_occupant\_only\_bonus} on, with the raid economy
locked down (\texttt{raid\_cost} maxed, \texttt{raid\_success\_prob}
minned, held-apple stream zeroed).  Round 2 ($\to 2732$; accepted)
is the qualitatively interesting move: Blue \emph{toggled}
\texttt{plaza\_occupant\_only\_bonus} \emph{back off} after observing
Red parking in plaza to harvest the now-occupant-only payouts.
Blue's recorded rationale: \emph{``Disabling occupant-only restores
global welfare leakage back to Blue; enabling
\texttt{plaza\_local\_clean\_gate} ties plaza payouts to the
collector's own river hygiene, which Blue maintains by role and
Red ignores.''}  Round 3 ($\to 5117$; accepted) is numeric polish
over the round-2 structural choice.  Raids per episode collapsed
from $\sim$15 to 0.0 from round 2 onward.  Of the 12 numerics
edited in the final patch, 8 are at a grammar bound; the other 4
are within $20\%$ of one — the binding constraint on the numeric
component is the grammar, not the LLM.

\paragraph{Trajectory 3 — \texttt{cu} $\times$ default (single-shot structural insight).}
Cleanup's only structural toggle is \texttt{beam\_enabled}; turning
it off disables tagging entirely.  Round 0 turned it off on the
first attempt, with the rationale: \emph{``Blue's cooperator never
fires the beam, so disabling tagging is a free structural win — it
removes the asymmetric weapon Red uses to suppress the worst-off
Blue harvester.''}  Round 3 reaffirms the choice: \emph{``Beam
stays off — the frozen cooperator never fires it, so enabling it
only arms Red.''}  Rounds 2 and 3 are numeric refinement
(\texttt{clean\_cost} slashed, \texttt{threshold\_restoration}
maxed, \texttt{apple\_respawn\_prob} doubled) over the same
toggle-off baseline; 2 of the 5 accepted numerics fully saturate.
Round 1 was lost to a JSON-parsing failure on Blue's response and
the framework correctly fell back to the identity patch — at $T=4$
this represents one wasted Blue turn out of four.  The
$\lambda^\star = 0.4$ invasion barrier is the most permissive of
the three runs.

\paragraph{Hypothesis status.}
The plan registered four pre-experiment hypotheses; we summarize
their status with the caveat that $N = 1$ trajectory per
(game, cooperator):
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{H1 (existence of robust mechanisms):}  mixed.  Two
        of three runs lifted $V_\lambda^\star$ above the round-0
        baseline; one ran flat.
  \item \textbf{H2 (fairness $>$ welfare for invasion robustness):}
        \emph{inverted under our grammar.}  The efficiency
        cooperator strictly outperformed the maximin cooperator on
        \texttt{nested\_commons}.  The new toggles' role
        preconditions favor static specialists and break under
        Latin-square rotation — robustness here is a property of
        the joint (cooperator, mechanism), not of the cooperator
        alone.
  \item \textbf{H3 (structural rule edits emerge):}  \emph{yes on
        the two non-trivial runs.}  Final accepted patches each
        carry at least one toggle, and Blue uses the toggle
        action-space deliberately, including a within-trajectory
        reversal on \texttt{nc} $\times$ efficiency round 2.
        Caveat: structural toggles \emph{ride on top of} numeric
        saturation rather than substituting for it; the cleanest
        H3 falsifier (toggles-off ablation) has not yet been run.
  \item \textbf{H4 (cross-env transfer):}  not directly tested —
        each env's grammar is bespoke.  The same \emph{kind} of
        toggle move (asymmetric-rule disable) appears across both
        successful runs.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Efficiency-metric anomaly.}
The Perolat efficiency $U = \tfrac{1}{T}\sum_{i,t} r_t^{(i)}$
computed against the \emph{final} accepted env lifts substantially
in both successful runs: $15.13 \to 85.73$ ($5.67\times$) on
\texttt{nc} $\times$ efficiency, $1.13 \to 2.08$ ($1.84\times$) on
\texttt{cu}.  The patched env is therefore strictly richer in
self-play, not only better at resisting invasion.  More striking:
on the final \texttt{cu} env, $U$ \emph{grows monotonically} with
$\lambda$ ($U \in \{2.08,\, 2.65,\, 2.87,\, 2.87,\, 3.42\}$ at
$\lambda \in \{0,\, 0.1,\, 0.2,\, 0.25,\, 0.4\}$); on the final
\texttt{nc} env it is flat across $\lambda \in [0, 0.25]$ and
dips slightly only at $\lambda = 0.4$.  Mechanism: on
\texttt{cu}, with \texttt{beam\_enabled = False}, Red contributes
apple harvest with no friendly-fire downside while the cooperator
under-harvests on the apple-rich post-patch env.  On \texttt{nc}
the symmetric public-goods plaza channel renders exploitation and
participation operationally identical.  This is the
\emph{saturated public-good} failure mode: once Blue's edits make
the env rich enough, exploit and cooperate become
indistinguishable.  Cooperator-relative efficiency — the fraction
of theoretical per-step ceiling realized by the population —
would be a more discriminating headline metric than absolute $U$.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the trajectories show.}
The framework is mechanically sound and surfaces interpretable
artifacts: each successful run's final $\Egram$-diff is a short,
human-readable patch with at least one structural toggle and a
small set of numeric edits.  Blue's recorded rationales correctly
identify cooperator–Red asymmetries (the BEAM monopoly on
\texttt{cleanup}) and adapt non-monotonically when Red exploits a
previous Blue choice (the round-2 reversal on \texttt{nc} $\times$
efficiency).  These behaviors are not guaranteed by the algorithm
— they depend on the proposer's reasoning quality — and serve as
positive evidence that the formulation rewards mechanism-design
reasoning rather than blind parameter search.

\paragraph{Cooperator–mechanism interaction.}
The maximin-vs-efficiency divergence on a single grammar is the
panel's most paper-worthy observation.  Standard fairness vs.\
welfare arguments would predict the maximin cooperator to be more
invasion-robust under selfish-defector Red; we observe the
opposite.  The mechanism is structural, not statistical: the
toggles' preconditions (per-clan cleanliness, plaza co-location)
are satisfied by static specialists and broken by role rotation.
This is precisely the interaction the ARIMD formulation is
designed to surface — robustness as a joint property of cooperator
and rule-set, not of either in isolation.

\paragraph{What the trajectories do not yet show.}
With $N = 1$ outer trajectory per cell, every quantitative claim
is single-sample.  The single critical missing experiment is the
\emph{numeric-only ablation}: re-run each successful trajectory
with toggles disabled in $\Egram$.  If $V_\lambda^\star$ recovers
to within statistical noise of the toggles-on result, H3 is
falsified — the toggles were decoration on a numeric saturation
that did the actual work.  The ablation is cheap (a one-line
grammar change, identical loop) and is the highest-information
next experiment.

\section{Limitations and threats to validity}
\label{sec:limitations}
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Single trajectory per cell.}  No outer-loop seed
        variation.  Cleanup's round-0 structural insight in
        particular could be lucky — a different rng seed might
        produce a Red that does not exhibit the BEAM monopoly Blue
        exploited in round 0.
  \item \textbf{Numeric saturation.}  $8/12$ accepted numerics on
        \texttt{nc} $\times$ efficiency and $2/5$ on \texttt{cu}
        sit at a grammar bound; the bounds, not the LLM, are the
        binding constraint on the numeric component of the search.
  \item \textbf{Self-play $U$ lift.}  The final env's $U_0$
        exceeds the original env's $U_0$ by $1.8$–$5.7\times$ on
        the successful runs, consistent with Blue \emph{richening}
        the env rather than only hardening it.  Reporting
        cooperator-relative $U$, or $V_\lambda - V_0$ at fixed
        grammar, would distinguish the two regimes.
  \item \textbf{Single LLM oracle pair.}  Blue $=$ Opus-4.7,
        Red $=$ Gemini-3.1-pro.  A different oracle assignment
        could swap which side is more imaginative; we have no
        ablation here.
  \item \textbf{Parse fragility.}  One JSON-parse failure on
        \texttt{cu} round 1 cost $25\%$ of the search budget at
        $T=4$.  Structured-output mode for $\mathcal{D}$ would
        close this.
  \item \textbf{Diagnostic.}  The stability-gap diagnostic of
        \S\ref{sec:stackelberg} returns $0.000$ on the two
        successful runs despite per-round $V_\lambda$ jumps of
        $\sim$1800 reward; the implementation is buggy and the
        reported values are not load-bearing.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
ARIMD instantiates a Stackelberg meta-game between an LLM
env-designer and an LLM exploit-policy synthesizer over a bounded
grammar of mechanism rules.  We presented the formalization, the
loop algorithm, and three small-scale empirical trajectories.
The framework recovers structural-rule edits (\texttt{beam\_enabled}
disabled on \texttt{cleanup}; \texttt{plaza\_local\_clean\_gate}
flipped on \texttt{nested\_commons}) with interpretable Blue
rationales, and surfaces a non-trivial cooperator–mechanism
interaction on a single grammar.  The critical near-term
experiment is the toggles-off ablation; until that is run, the
present results are encouraging but not conclusive evidence that
the structural action-space dominates parameter saturation.
